After 20 years in rehabilitation leadership, I finally have a name for what separates good clinicians from exceptional ones.

It's not talent. It's not experience alone.

It's the ability to work 𝗱𝗲𝗲𝗽𝗹𝘆.

▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬

📌 I've been reading Deep Work by Cal Newport — and Chapter 1 hit differently.

Newport defines Deep Work as:

❝ Professional activities performed in a state of distraction-free concentration that push your cognitive capabilities to their limit — creating new value that is hard to replicate. ❞

The opposite? 𝗦𝗵𝗮𝗹𝗹𝗼𝘄 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸.

Emails. Endless meetings. Constant notifications.

We've all mistaken being busy for being productive.

▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬

🧮 Newport's High-Performance Equation:

𝗛𝗶𝗴𝗵-𝗤𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝗢𝘂𝘁𝗽𝘂𝘁 = 𝗧𝗶𝗺𝗲 𝗦𝗽𝗲𝗻𝘁 × 𝗜𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗻𝘀𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝗼𝗳 𝗙𝗼𝗰𝘂𝘀

Two hours of deep focus beats eight hours of distracted work — every time.

▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬

🎯 In today's economy, three types of people win:

▪️ Those with rare, high-value skills
▪️ Those who are the best at what they do
▪️ Those with capital to invest

The first two? Both require one thing:

The ability to learn deeply and fast.

▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬

⚠️ The uncomfortable truth:

Deep work is becoming increasingly rare — and simultaneously more valuable.

Three enemies are killing our focus:

1. Instant messaging culture
2. Unnecessary meetings
3. The false metric: "busy = productive"

▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬

🏥 As a Rehabilitation Department Head, I see this daily.

The clinicians who produce exceptional outcomes aren't the ones answering every message instantly…

They're the ones who protect their cognitive space:

For assessment, for clinical reasoning, for genuine problem-solving.

Deep work isn't a luxury in healthcare…

It's a patient safety issue.
